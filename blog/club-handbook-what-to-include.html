What to Include in a Club Handbook

A six-page checklist for the running handbook of a small club, society or association — what belongs on each page, what to leave out, and example copy you can paste in and just change the brackets.

A club handbook has three jobs: tell new members what the club is, tell officers what they are supposed to do, and tell everyone when things happen. A handbook that does those three things gets used. One that tries to do more gets filed and forgotten.

Page 1 — Cover and at-a-glance

The front page answers the four questions a new member asks in the first five minutes: what is this club, when and where do we meet, who runs it, how do I join. Put those four in boxes, not in paragraphs.

Example copy — change the brackets

[Club Name] · Running Handbook · [Year] Season

What we are: [one-sentence mission]
We meet: [day], [time], at [location]
Contact: [name] · [email] · [phone]
Membership: [dues amount] per [year], due [date]

Page 2 — Officers and their jobs

One line per role: what the president does, what the secretary does, who holds the money. Write duties as verbs — “calls the meeting,” “sends the minutes within 48 hours,” “signs the cheque.” Vague role descriptions are where clubs get stuck.

Page 3 — Meetings and the year at a glance

Regular meeting times first, then the season’s fixed events in a calendar grid. Quorum rules and how to call a special meeting go at the bottom of this page, not buried in bylaws nobody reads.

Page 4 — Membership and money

Who can join, what joining costs, when dues are due, what members get for it. On money, be exact: amounts, dates, and what happens if someone is late. Most club arguments are about one of these three things.

Page 5 — Rules that actually get used

Summarize the bylaws in plain language; keep the full legal text as a separate file, not inside the printed pack. Include only rules members actually encounter: conduct at meetings, voting, guests, use of the space and equipment.

Page 6 — Back page

Contacts, a sign-up sheet for help, and the club’s online home. A printed handbook should end with a way to act; otherwise it is just paper.

When to finish it

Before the first meeting of the season. A handbook handed out at the kickoff gets read; a handbook emailed in October about last October gets deleted.

Frequently asked

How long should a small club handbook be?

Six printed pages is about right for a club under a hundred members. If it runs to twenty pages, the bylaws have eaten the handbook.

Do we need a designer?

No. The hard parts — type sizes, spacing, page order — are already decided and tested on real printers. Replace the bracketed text and print.

PDF or Word?

Word to edit, PDF to print. Edit in Word, then print the matching PDF at 100% so the type stays exactly where it was designed.
