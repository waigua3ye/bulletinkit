School Play Program: What to Put on It

The six pages a parent-organized school production actually needs — cover, cast, crew, scene order, a note from the director, and the thank-you page. With example copy you can fill in the night before.

School programs fail in the same three ways every year: the cast list is misspelled, the “helpful parents” list forgets someone, and the print shop deadline gets missed at 11 p.m. the night before. A fixed layout fixes the third problem, and a checklist fixes the first two.

Page 1 — Cover

Title, school, date, time, venue, ticket price if there is one. That is it. A cover with twelve lines is a poster; a cover with five lines is a program.

Example copy — change the brackets

[School Name] presents

“[Title of the Play]”
[Month] [Day], [Year] · [Time] · [Venue / Room]
Directed by [Name] · Tickets: [Price / Free]

Page 2 — Cast

Characters in order of appearance, child’s name after each role. Spell every name from the class roster, not from memory or from a text message. This is the page parents photograph; a misspelled name here is a three-week conversation.

Page 3 — Crew

Stage manager, lights, sound, costumes, props, set, and the parents who built things. List everyone who did anything, even “snack table.” The crew page is where quiet helpers get their moment; leaving someone off it is the number one source of hurt feelings.

Page 4 — Scene order and music

Scenes in order with the cast on stage for each, plus any songs with who performs them. For a kindergarten play with twenty-three children, this page is the whole show for the grandparents in row three.

Page 5 — Director’s note and rehearsal schedule

Four sentences about the production, then the call times: arrive, costume check, curtain. Parents need times more than they need prose.

Page 6 — Thank yous and house rules

Thank the venue, the volunteers, and the sponsors in one block. Then the house rules in three lines: when photography is allowed, where the children sit, when the doors open. Written once, enforced all night.

Print tips

  • Print at 100% scale on US Letter (A4 if outside the US). Programs stapled in half-sheet booklets feel twice as finished as loose pages.
  • Black and white is fine. A clean grayscale program reads better under auditorium light than a color one printed on a home inkjet.
  • Print a spare stack. Ten extra copies in a bag beats a panicked midnight print run.
  • Proofread the cast and crew pages twice, from the roster, by two different people.

Frequently asked

When should the program be printed?

Names are final after the last rehearsal. Print the week before, not the night before — the night before is when typos survive.

What size should a school play program be?

US Letter folded in half (A4 folded in half outside the US) is the standard: sturdy, stackable, fits a pocket. Full page open, half page folded.

Do we need a photo on the cover?

A rehearsal photo works if it prints cleanly in grayscale. If your printer cannot handle photos, leave the cover typographic — nobody ever complained about a clean title page.
