Church Bulletin Cover Ideas That Still Work on a Black-and-White Copier

Twelve cover approaches, chosen because they survive greyscale printing, a cheap copier, and being folded into a pocket.

Most cover inspiration online assumes colour printing. Most churches print in black and white on a copier that is older than the youth group. These twelve ideas are picked for that reality: they read at 30% grey, they survive a fold, and they take ten minutes to produce.

Type-only covers (the safest)

1. One huge word. The season or the sermon title, set at 96–120 pt, filling the top third. Nothing else but the date. Works because it is readable from across a room.

2. Date as the design. "NOVEMBER 9" set very large, with the church name small underneath. Cheap, modern, and impossible to get wrong.

3. A single verse, ragged right. Four lines of scripture in the lower half, generous space above. Leave the top third empty — the emptiness is what makes it look designed.

One-image covers

4. A window. Any window from your own building, shot straight on, converted to greyscale with contrast pushed up. Instantly recognisable as "this place".

5. A hand. One hand on a shoulder, on a table, holding a cup. Hands photograph well in greyscale and carry more warmth than faces.

6. A door. Plain, slightly open. Works for Advent, welcome Sundays, and any sermon about invitation.

7. A chair. An empty chair in the room where you meet — reads as "there is a place for you" without saying it.

Shape and rule covers

8. A single thick rule. One horizontal line at the optical centre, church name above, date below. This is the most under-used cover in church printing.

9. A circle and a word. A thin circle with the season inside it, off-centre. Prints cleanly at any size.

10. A repeating grid. Small crosses or dots in a 6×6 grid, one cell filled in. Abstract, and it looks intentional.

What to avoid

11. Not this: four-point clip-art stars, drop shadows on type, and a rainbow gradient behind the title. All three turn to mud on a copier.

12. Not this either: a photo with the sermon title laid over the busy part. Put type on the empty part of the image or on a solid band, never on faces or foliage.

The greyscale test

Before you commit, convert the cover to greyscale and look at it at 40% size on screen. If you cannot read the title at thumbnail size, it will not work folded in a pocket either.

Specs that matter more than the design

  • Image resolution: 300 dpi at final print size. A 1000 px-wide image across 8.5 in is about 118 dpi — it will look soft.
  • Contrast, not colour: greyscale conversion kills reds and greens. Push contrast +20% before printing.
  • Keep 0.25 in clear at every edge, or the copier will crop your design.

Frequently asked

Do I need a designer? No. Ideas 1, 2, 3 and 8 need nothing but Word and the confidence to leave space empty.

Can I use photos from the internet? Only with a licence that covers print. Unsplash and Pexels are fine; a random image from a search result is not.

Should the cover match the season colours? Only if you print in colour. In greyscale, a "purple Advent cover" and a "red Pentecost cover" are the same grey.
