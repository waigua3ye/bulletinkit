What to Include in a Church Bulletin

A six-page checklist for the congregation that prints its own bulletin — what goes on each page, what to cut, and example copy you can paste in and just change the brackets.

A small-church bulletin has four jobs, and only four: tell people what is happening, order the service, remember the people, and ask for a response. Everything that does not do one of those four jobs is filler. Here is how the six pages should carry those jobs.

Page 1 — Cover and order of service

The front page is what people hold while they walk in. Keep it to the service itself: date, service time, the order of worship with who leads each item, and the preacher’s name. Scripture readings go in the order they occur, not in Bible order.

Example copy — change the brackets

Sunday, [Month] [Day] · 10:00 a.m. · [Church Name]

Call to Worship — [Leader]
Hymn: [Hymn Title and Number]
Prayer of Confession — [Leader]
Reading: [Book Chapter:Verse] — [Reader]
Sermon: “[Title]” — [Pastor]
Response Hymn · Benediction

Page 2 — Announcements

Put the five most-used items first: kids’ schedule, midweek study, service times that changed, the big upcoming event, and giving. One line each: what, when, where, who to ask. If an announcement needs a paragraph, it is probably two announcements or not needed.

Page 3 — Prayers and people

Birthdays, new members, people in hospital, people being baptized, the bereaved. This page is why people keep old bulletins. A simple three-column list works better than prose.

Page 4 — The month at a glance

A calendar grid for the month. Put recurring items in first (every Wednesday study, every first Friday), then the one-offs. People photograph this page more than any other.

Page 5 — Ways to serve

Set up, music, greeting, children’s room, cleaning. Each role gets one line and a sign-up line with a name and phone or email. A bulletin that never asks for help trains people to never volunteer.

Page 6 — Back page

A short quote, a hymn verse, the parking and accessibility notes, and the office contact. Keep it quiet; this page is the exhale.

Print settings that actually matter

  • Scale: 100%, not “fit to page.” Fit-to-page shrinks type until the elderly cannot read it.
  • Body text 11–12 pt. Headings 14 pt or more. Gray backgrounds eat ink and blur at the copier.
  • Design in black and white. Color prints fine, but a bulletin that works in grayscale never fails on the office printer.
  • A4 or US Letter. US churches and schools print Letter; most of the world prints A4. Keep both sizes on hand so nobody has to re-lay-out anything.

When to finish it

By Wednesday for a Sunday bulletin. Later than that and the proofreader is you, at 6 a.m., at the church copier.

Frequently asked

How many pages is a small-church bulletin?

Four to six. Six gives you room for the calendar and the volunteer page without crowding the service order. Fewer than four and announcements start eating the service page.

Can I make it look professional without design skills?

Yes — that is the whole point of a template. Pick a layout, replace the bracketed text, print. The layout decisions (type sizes, spacing, page order) are already made and tested on real printers.

PDF or Word?

Word to edit, PDF to print. Edit in Word, then print the matching PDF at 100% so the type stays exactly where it was designed.
