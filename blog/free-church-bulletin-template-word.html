Free Church Bulletin Templates: What "Free" Actually Costs You

Most free bulletin templates fail in the same three places — margins, fonts, and the order of service. Here is what to check before you spend a Sunday morning fixing one.

Search "free church bulletin template" and you get thousands of results. Almost all of them are one of three things: a screenshot of a bulletin, a Google Docs file someone shared in 2014, or a template that only works in one specific program. None of those are wrong, but each one costs you something different. Knowing which cost you are paying is the whole trick.

The three costs of a free template

Cost 1 — the margin is wrong. Home printers cannot print to the edge of the paper. Most consumer printers need at least 0.25 inch (6 mm) on every side. A template designed at 0.1 inch looks fine on screen and clips your prayer list in print. Check the template's page setup before you type anything into it.

Cost 2 — the font is not installed. A template that uses a paid font will silently substitute something else on your machine, and the layout that looked balanced will reflow. If the file does not name a font you can find for free on Google Fonts or that ships with Windows or macOS, assume it will break.

Cost 3 — it is an image, not a document. A very common free download is a flattened PDF or a JPG "so you can see the layout". Those are useful for ideas and useless for editing. If you cannot click into a line of text and change it, it is a picture, not a template.

What a usable template must have

  • An editable text layer — Word (.docx) or Google Docs, not a flattened PDF.
  • Both paper sizes — US Letter for the US, A4 for the UK, Ireland, Australia and New Zealand. A Letter file printed on A4 loses about 18 mm of height.
  • Named styles, not manual formatting — so changing one heading does not require reformatting six pages.
  • A print-ready PDF twin — for the weeks when you just need it printed and out the door.
The check that saves the most time

Open the template. Press Ctrl+A then look at the font box. If it shows one font name, the template is built properly. If it shows a mix of three or four, every paragraph was formatted by hand and you will inherit that mess.

Frequently asked

Can I use a free template for a church that meets in a school? Yes — the template does not care where you meet. What changes is the printer. School copiers usually handle full-bleed better than home printers, but still keep 0.25 in margins.

Do I need a design program? No. Word or Google Docs is enough for a six-page bulletin. Design software helps with covers, not with orders of service.

What about Canva? Canva is good for covers. It is awkward for a four-column order of service that has to change every week, because you edit on a canvas instead of in a document.
